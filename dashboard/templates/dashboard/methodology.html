METHODOLOGY

How PLEIADES builds one consistent fire record.

A transparent overview of the data, the harmonization approach and the limits of what the platform can tell you.

Data sources

MODIS

Long record, coarser pixels

Active-fire detections at roughly 1 km resolution, available for more than two decades.

VIIRS

Newer, finer detail

Active-fire detections at roughly 375 m resolution, able to see smaller and cooler fires, available since 2012.

Because the sensors differ in resolution and sensitivity, raw counts from one cannot be compared directly with the other. That is the gap PLEIADES closes.

Approach

  1. IngestLoad NASA FIRMS active-fire CSV files for MODIS and VIIRS (import_firms). Low-confidence detections are excluded by default and can be included from the calendar.Live
  2. Common gridEvery detection is snapped to a 0.05° cell (about 5 km), coarser than both sensors' pixels. We count active cell-days instead of pixels, which removes the effect of VIIRS reporting several pixels for one fire.Live
  3. Cross-calibrateOn the years both sensors fly, the factor MODIS cell-days / VIIRS cell-days rescales VIIRS onto the MODIS scale. It is estimated for your area, with a region-wide fallback for very small areas. The weekly correlation between the sensors is shown as a quality check.Live
  4. HarmonizeThe daily index uses rescaled VIIRS where it exists (better sensitivity) and MODIS before that, giving one series with no artificial jump when VIIRS starts.Live
  5. Calendar & critical periodsActivity is binned in 7-day weeks. Weeks that reach at least half of the typical peak are merged into critical periods (fire seasons).Live
  6. Unusual conditionsEach week of each year is compared with the same week in all other years (leave-one-year-out). Weeks 2 or more standard deviations above typical are flagged, with a Poisson-like floor on the deviation so small counts do not produce huge scores.Live
  7. AssistLet members query the record and risk indicators through an AI assistant.Planned

Limits & honesty

Satellite hotspots show detected heat, not confirmed damage. Cloud cover and overpass timing can hide fires. Risk indicators are estimates built from historical patterns and weather data, not guarantees. Harmonized values are in MODIS-equivalent units, not hectares burned. Official fire authorities remain the source of truth for emergencies.
